Why most copy fails the second read

Kieran Fielder · 4 min read · Sample post — template preview

Anyone can write copy that survives the first read. The first read is fast, generous, half-skimmed on a phone — it forgives almost everything. The sale falls apart on the second read: the one where your prospect comes back an hour later, defences up, looking for a reason to say no.

The second read is a stress test

On the second read, adjectives collapse. "Effortless", "seamless", "powerful" — the reader doesn't feel them twice. What survives is anything with weight: a number, a mechanism, a named customer, a specific bad day the product prevents. That's why the strongest line in most of my drafts is usually the least decorated one.

"Adjectives get you the first read. Evidence gets you the reply."

So when I edit, I do the second read on purpose. Same copy, next morning, phone in hand, pretending I'm busy and mildly annoyed — because that's exactly who's reading it.

Steal the checklist

Cut every claim you can't back within one sentence. Replace one adjective per paragraph with a fact. Read the call-to-action as if you've already been interrupted twice. If it still earns the click, ship it.

/Case Study

Writing an Educational YouTube Script Using AI

How I turned a brochure-sounding first draft into a verified, beginner-ready 10-minute video script for a financial education channel, with hallucinations corrected.

Money Over TimePrompt EngineeringYouTube Script3 Prompts + 1 Edit Pass

Brief

A script that earns trust

Money Over Time teaches beginner investors. Their audience is middle class and mostly self-taught. The brief was to explain diversification in a way that felt like advice from a knowledgeable friend, not a compliance department. Every factual claim needed systematic verification.

Prompts

You are a Financial Educator with a masters degree in finance and 10+ years of experience as a financial advisor for an investment firm... [full detailed persona prompt]
Role: You are a financial educator writing a Youtube Script for a channel called Money Over Time. Focus on teaching and clear actional instruction instead of metaphors... [full structured prompt]

Hallucination Audit

Factual verification

Claim from v2 ScriptVerdictCorrection Applied
"Large-cap companies valued above £8 billion"MisleadingChanged to index-based definition: FTSE 100 / S&P 500 thresholds vary
"Small-cap companies valued below £2 billion"MisleadingChanged to percentile / index-based definition
"US stocks went sideways for nearly ten years while international markets produced solid returns"Partially falseRewrote to specify emerging markets only
"When stock markets dropped sharply in early 2020, high-quality government bonds actually increased in value"True but incompleteAdded caveat about 2022 exception
"Brexit hit UK markets hard in 2016 while much of the world was unaffected"OverstatedChanged to "dipped briefly but recovered faster than UK"

Final Script

HOOK
"Most investors don't lose money because they picked the wrong company. They lose money because they put too much money into too few things."
RE-HOOK
"Let me give you a real example. Imagine someone called James. He's 34. He earns around £45,000 a year... When that stock drops 60%, James loses over £10,000. Years of saving. Gone in months."
STEP 1: Asset Classes
"Stocks. When you buy a stock, you buy a small ownership stake in a company. Bonds. When you buy a bond, you lend money to a government or company."
STEP 2: Sectors and Sizes
"They buy ten different stocks — and feel diversified. But all ten are tech companies. That's not diversification. That's concentration with extra steps."
STEP 3: Geographic Spread
"The UK stock market makes up about 4% of the global market. That means if you only invest in British companies, you are missing 96% of the world."
STEP 4: Time Horizon
"Money you need within five years should not be in stocks. In 2022, the S&P 500 fell by roughly 19%. If you needed that money then, you sold at a loss."
CONCLUSION AND CTA
"Diversification isn't one thing. It's four connected decisions. Download the Portfolio Planning Worksheet in the description."

Reflection

In my initial prompt the attempt was to ensure the prompt was as context rich as possible stuffing the persona role with a great amount of detail. This backfired in the end. I realise now that constraints and a more organised prompt structure is preferable. Also I could have pre-empted a lot of the hallucinations and applied accounted for this within my initial prompt. These pre-empted constraints could be used to create batch prompts for future projects to ensuring a more efficient workflow.

Human Words · Email and Direct-Response · Fintech

Pulse Financials Launch Email

A direct-response launch email for a digital bank built specifically for freelancers, side-hustlers, and small business owners, making tax stress disappear.

Client
Pulse Financials
Format
Launch Email
Goal
Account Sign-ups

Brief

Make the reader feel understood

Self-employed people passionate about their work but stressed about financial admin. The email needed to make the reader feel understood, then position Pulse as the simple, stress-free solution. Tone: empathetic but confident, conversational not corporate.

The Work

Open before January 31st

Hey [name],

You did not start your business to become a dead-end professional in a boring job. You started it for passion. For freedom. Because you love the feeling when you have just made another sale.

But right now that feeling probably comes with knots in your stomach. Because every pound that hits your account gets mixed up with money for Costa trips, weekends away, and that loan from Grandma. And somewhere in your mind is the sinking feeling the tax man will want his cut.

So we built Pulse. A new kind of digital bank built for people like you. When you open a Pulse Builder Account, you get a Pulse Pot. Tell it to save 20% for taxes. Every time a payment lands, Pulse automatically sweeps that 20% into your pot. When tax season rolls around, the money is just there.

Meet Sarah. A photographer in Columbus, Ohio. Before Pulse, she used spreadsheets and lay awake dreading tax time. Last year, with Pulse, she saved $4,000 automatically. Now it is her "peace of mind button."

Our offer for you: When you open your Builder Account, we will include a complimentary 30-minute tax readiness call with one of our partner accountants. No pressure. No sales pitch. Just straight answers.

Imagine a stress-free April. That feeling starts today. Go to pulsebank.com. Open your Builder Account in minutes. Give your future self some peace of mind.

James Keller, Founder, Pulse Financials

P.S. One account. One automatic sweep. Zero tax stress. That is Pulse.

Reflection

The question at the top immediately connects with deeper motivation. Freelancers don't start for the admin. They start for freedom, passion, control. By naming that first, I built identity alignment before introducing any product. The Sarah story offers social proof without a formal testimonial. The complimentary tax call lowers the barrier. Circling back to the emotional opening leaves readers with a vision of relief, not a feature list.

Human Words · Product Copy · Automotive

Hyundai IONIQ 5 N Product Feature Copy

Balancing emotional appeal with technical detail for an electric performance vehicle. Confident, cinematic, and precise.

Client
Hyundai
Format
Product Feature Copy

Brief

Excite and inform

Product feature copy that sits between a spec sheet and a lifestyle ad. Car enthusiasts want numbers; early EV adopters want cutting-edge tech. The copy had to excite and inform without tipping too far either direction.

The Work

Hyundai IONIQ 5 N
Judge This Book By Its Cover. Pixelated, Powered, Potent.
The Hyundai IONIQ 5 N is your all-access pass to the future of performance. Its pixelated design is a digital signature blending retro with cutting-edge.
This is a vehicle designed for the journey, offering a fully customizable driving experience where you command the steering weight, torque distribution and even the G-Force at leisure.
It is engineered for the destination, arriving with undeniable presence thanks to its aggressive stance, unique black N badge, and essential wing-spoiler. The freedom continues off the line, with its 800V high-performance architecture enabling a staggering 641 horsepower and ultra-fast charging.
Every detail serves a purpose. The front air intakes cooling the brakes ensure all speed, no overheating. The massive 21-inch forged aluminium wheels translate power into improved traction and cornering grip.
This is performance, perfected and packaged with breathtaking style. Discover the IONIQ 5 N, where every drive is a highlight reel.

Reflection

The biggest challenge was balancing emotion with technical specs. Car enthusiasts want the numbers, but they also want to feel something about the car. My approach was to use confident, cinematic language to build desire, then anchor every emotional claim with a concrete detail. When I said "undeniable presence," I backed it with the black N badge and wing spoiler. When I talked about performance, I gave them the horsepower, the charging architecture, and the wheel specifications. The hype feels earned because the proof is right there.

Human Words · Brand Story · Trade and Industry

UK Wholesaler Brand Story

From a single unit in Salford to five branches across the country, a grounded, trustworthy origin story for tradespeople who value reliability over marketing fluff.

Client
UK Wholesaler
Format
Brand Story / About Us

Brief

Tell the origin story, demonstrate growth

A brand story for a UK wholesaler that started as a single unit in Salford and grew to five branches. The audience is tradespeople who value reliability, speed, and trust over marketing fluff. The story needed to feel human and grounded, not polished and distant.

The Work

In July of 2004 our founders spent a whole day chasing down basic parts for the job. This day taught them a lot and led them to this belief: that wholesalers should be partners to the flow of the job and not hurdles to get over.

So, they opened a single unit in Salford, stocking the reliable, hard-working components they trusted themselves.

For fifteen years they have remained close to those same principles of trust and pragmatism that they started with. And so they worked hard to earn the trust of local tradespersons in the area. From that solid bedrock of trust and reputation they were able to successfully expand across the UK.

Today, they are thriving, with five branches from Winsford to Sidcup and beyond, and are still the same dedicated partner. And still they are looking to open new doors in the future in locations around the UK.

Reflection

I opened with the specific anecdote about the founders spending a whole day chasing down basic parts. This was intentional. Tradespeople know exactly what that feels like. A wasted day on a simple job because the supply chain failed. By starting there, I proved the founders understand the audience's reality before making any claims about their business. Tradespeople do not respond to corporate language. They respond to trust, reliability, and evidence.

Human Words · Google Ads · Fintech

Vault Savings App Google Ad

A micro-savings campaign that reframes putting money aside as something you barely notice, turning reluctant savers into Vault users with a single search ad.

Client
Vault
Format
Google Search Ad
Objective
App Downloads

Brief

Make saving feel invisible

Vault is an automatic micro-savings app. The target audience is not disciplined investors; it is the person who wants to save but has never been able to stick to it. The brief: write a Google Search Ad that removes psychological friction entirely. Do not sell saving. Sell the absence of effort.

The Ad

Ad · vault.app/automatic-savings · Sponsored
Micro Saves — Major Goals
Save Without Noticing | Vault Automatic Savings
Instantly save money at the moment of transaction. You don't have to stress it. Micro-savings means you don't have to feel it sting at the end of the month. No big deal sacrifice.

Strategic Thinking

Most automatic savings apps double down on the technical aspects of their application. The simplicity, the uniformity, the ease. This is good enough. Yet in writing this ad, I didn't want to bypass the real human frustration of muddling through the motivation and the discipline involved when working towards a saving goal. This is the desire behind the intent when the user searches for a savings app which is automatic. The final line — "No big deal sacrifice" — is casual, conversational, and removes any sense of pressure. It mirrors exactly how the audience actually thinks about money.

Reflection

The biggest insight from this project was realising that the audience does not want to think about saving. They want to think about literally anything else. Most financial copy tries to educate. This ad tries to reassure. It says: "You do not have to become a different person to save money. You just need a system that works while you are not paying attention." The "No big deal sacrifice" line turns "I should probably start saving at some point" into something that feels immediately possible. That shift — from abstract intention to concrete action — is what conversion copy actually does.
